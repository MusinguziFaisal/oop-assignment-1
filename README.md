## Summary of findings


1. **Population.** Wakiso grows fastest (CAGR 6.5 %). No single model wins everywhere: exponential growth is best for Kampala and Wakiso, the calibrated Fibonacci-ratio model for Gulu and Mbarara, and a linear trend for Jinja (decelerating). Wakiso overtakes Kampala by 2029 and the five districts need about 4,650 extra classrooms. The raw Fibonacci-ratio model implies about +62 %/year, which is not defensible.
2. **Micro-grid.** det(A) = -5 and cond(A) = 5.8, so the system is well-conditioned; a +/-5 % demand error moved the dispatch by up to 28 %, matching the condition-number bound. 6 of 30 days were infeasible (negative battery) and were handled with NNLS (about 1 % of demand left unmet). The battery is the more volatile source (CV 0.85 vs 0.40). Monthly cost is about UGX 225,000.
3. **Fish stock.** MSY (rK/4 = 1,000 t/week) is reached at h = 0.20. Harvesting harder (h = 0.30) lowers the equilibrium stock to 2,500 t and revenue by about 17 %. Revenue risk (CV) is Low in all scenarios, with 5 % VaR about 15-16 % of expected revenue; the real danger at high h is biological. An 8-week closed season costs 11-15 % of revenue at h <= 0.20 but raises it at h = 0.30.
4. **Rainfall.** Kampala is wettest (1,600 mm), Mbarara driest (1,040 mm) and bimodal. Cosine similarity rates all regions as similar (0.75-0.89) although Pearson correlations are near zero. Kampala's illustrative series is detected as unimodal, which conflicts with its usual two seasons (data limitation).
5. **Taxi routes.** The Ntinda equilibrium fare is UGX 2,200 (current UGX 2,000 is 9 % lower, a shortage of 10 passengers per trip-hour). Over 10 days a last-value forecast (SES, alpha = 1) is best, but the evidence is thin. Every route needs one vehicle. On 60 synthetic days a seasonal-naive forecaster nearly halves the moving average's error.

## Data and sources

* Populations (Kampala, Wakiso, Gulu), rainfall, Ntinda passenger counts and all given figures are from the assignment brief and are **illustrative**. Mbarara and Jinja populations (Project 1) and the Entebbe and Mukono counts (Project 5) are illustrative additions. The micro-grid demand CSV, fish prices and the weekly taxi series are synthetic, generated with fixed seeds.
* Crop rainfall thresholds (Project 4): FAO, "Water and soil requirements", Table 2 (maize 500-800 mm and beans 300-500 mm per growing period), <https://www.fao.org/3/u3160e/u3160e04.htm>; Uganda Coffee Development Authority coffee handbook, section 2.4 (robusta 1,200-1,800 mm a year, spread over about 9 months), <https://www.ugandacoffee.go.ug/file-download/download/public/42>. The 4- and 3-month season lengths used to convert to mm/month are my assumptions.
* **Extensions attempted:** P1 bootstrap prediction intervals (>= 1,000 resamples) and a Fibonacci-ratio critique; P2 hybrid 3x3 grid and Monte Carlo sensitivity; P3 closed season; P4 box plots, which use **synthetic** multi-year data because real NASA POWER / UNMA data were not available here (the notebook says so); P5 60-day seasonal experiment.
